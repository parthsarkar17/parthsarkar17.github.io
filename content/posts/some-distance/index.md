Arjun liked rainy days like the ones they’d been getting lately. The brown sweater he was wearing brought him some joy. He was seated at a window seat, tugging the sweater away from his collar when it got a bit scratchy. He occasionally ran a hand through his scattered hair, which, this morning, he noticed had begun to gray towards the roots. Damn, he thought when he saw that. Sitting here like this, though, was dignifying.

Dignifying? That was interesting, he thought. He’d gone up to the counter and ordered under a name that didn’t belong to him. He couldn’t exactly articulate why: convenience, maybe? That wasn’t quite right, but it was hard to put this sort of thing into words. The irony wasn’t lost on him— he just needed to get out of its way again.

He made his way up to the counter when the man behind it announced that Aaron’s shot of espresso was ready. It was in a paper cup, and, in small letters, someone had scrawled, across the side of it, those few letters that didn’t mean all that much to Arjun. That’s what was important.

“Aaron?” asked the man, with a smile. He was beautiful, with nice eyes, and he was actually a bit older than he had looked before. Yeah, Arjun responded. He took it back to his table. He hoped hadn’t been impolite.

It was good, like most coffees tend to be. Each sip was the same, and he couldn’t pin down anything to describe it. Didn’t matter too much, he thought. He tilted the paper cup and finished his espresso. He placed it down, and swiveled the cup so the word on the side of the cup faced away from him, instead revealing itself to the street outside. Arjun looked over his shoulder, hoping to catch a glimpse of the man; he was cleaning his machine’s steam wand with a cloth.

His father's eyes would widen with incredulity whenever Arjun did something that he couldn’t understand. Thinking about it now made Arjun’s chest harden and throat burn, where the coffee had just traced its path. He pushed himself to his feet, and, without giving a second glance to his paper cup, disposed of it. He decided not to look behind him again; he pushed open the door and reentered the world through the wet sidewalk in front of him.

Arjun began to walk towards his home. None of it really mattered anyway. The past couldn’t change, and he wasn’t even sure he’d feel like changing anything, even if he could. And, in truth, it didn’t even quite feel like a grudge anymore— those are supposed to make you angry, or something.

He was abruptly shoved onto his side. Without noticing, he had walked into a bike lane, and now there was a child on the ground with his back against a parked car. The bicycle that the child was riding had its handlebars entangled with a nearby parking meter.
“What the fuck, man. Are you fucking blind, or what?” The child was deeply angry, and tears immediately seemed to make their way to his eyes. 

He was too young for that rage. It blossomed into his fists, crashing, tearing, splitting this child’s mind. A senseless, violent, familiar dance, not started by Arjun, but one in which he inadvertently took part. God, I’m so sorry, are you alright? Arjun asked.

“Just watch where you’re going, man.” Barely able to contain his tears by this point, the child righted his bike and mounted it. He didn’t have any scrapes. Arjun watched for a moment as the child pedaled away slowly, steering his bike with only his left hand. The child’s right hand seemed poised near his face to catch any loose tears.

And then Arjun began walking, quickly, mindful to remain on the sidewalk. His anger was now measured, purposeful, deep. His tears were warm against his cheek, a comforting hand guiding him into an embrace. Past storefronts and more coffee shops, past people walking with and against his stride, until he reached his apartment. He let himself in and removed his shoes. A pot of coffee from early that morning remained in his brewer, still hot. He picked up his phone, dialing a number and, at once, reaching for a mug. It rang, rang once more, time hung for a moment, before a click.

“Hello?

His father’s voice. It had the same resoluteness. Arjun took a sip out of his mug. Damn, he thought. It was truthfully, sincerely, really good.

“Hi, Baba.”
